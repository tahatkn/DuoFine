Common Ground

A location-based social app for iOS and Android: find the best place to meet, chat in real time, and see where your friends are — designed, built and run by DuoFine.

Platform
iOS · Android
Stack
React Native · Django · PostgreSQL · WebSockets · Google Maps Platform
Role
Product, design, engineering, operations
Status
App Store · Google Play

The problem

Meeting friends across a city is a negotiation: who travels how far, by what means, to which kind of place, at what time. Every group chat has the same twenty messages before anyone leaves the house. Common Ground exists to skip them — take everyone's location and preferences, and propose the place that is fair, reachable and worth going to.

Under that friendly surface sits an unforgiving set of engineering constraints: live location from many devices, messaging that has to feel instant, venue data from a third-party platform, and a database that keeps growing with every conversation.

What we built

  • Venue discovery with filters for distance, transport mode, category and popularity, backed by Google Maps Platform data.
  • A common meeting point computed for two or more people from their live locations and how each of them is travelling.
  • Friends and live location so a plan can be made — and adjusted — while people are already on the move.
  • Real-time chat over WebSockets, with a history that stays fast as the message table grows (more on that below).
  • Bookmarks with personal labels, and W-W Deals: offers from partner businesses redeemed in-app with a code or QR.
  • Plus and Pro subscriptions through the App Store and Google Play, with a token system that meters discovery features.
  • Sign-in with email, Google or Apple, device-level checks against duplicate accounts, and account deletion from inside the app.

The hard problem: chat history at 700 ms

As the Message table grew, the query that loads a conversation drifted from instant to 700 ms at p95. EXPLAIN ANALYZE showed why: a sequential scan over the whole table followed by an in-memory Top-N sort. The fix was not more hardware but two compound B-tree indexes aligned with the exact shape of the query — equality columns first, the sort column last — so PostgreSQL could walk an already-ordered index backwards and stop after a hundred rows.

The result was 38 ms at p95, a 94.6% reduction, and a database that spends its CPU on something else. The full diagnosis is written up in Fixing a 700ms PostgreSQL Query: From Seq Scan to 38ms.

Operating it

Shipping a consumer app is the beginning of the work, not the end. Alongside the app itself, DuoFine runs the pieces that stores and regulators expect: the terms and privacy policy in sixteen languages, an account-deletion path that works with or without the app installed, and the account activation, e-mail change and password reset flows that the app hands off to the web.
